## Project Concept

The “brain” of my project is the player, who, through their actions, is able to change what they perceive depending on where they stand in the room. In an actual brain, depending on the context of the environment and what the brain attends to, two people can perceive different things. Like we talked about in class, two people sitting in the same room may perceive vastly different things depending on what they are attending to. Someone focusing their attention on a speaker will understand and perceive the speaker, while someone focusing their attention on their phone will not. 

In my project, attention and perception are exemplified very definitively. Depending on where the player stands in the room (simulating attention), the player will be able to see different things (emphasizing perceptual organization). The smaller objects that make up the sculpture have their own individual meanings associated with them. Getting up close to the sculpture allows the player to perceive these smaller objects and grasp their individual meanings. The player  is able to focus on them, and seeing the assorted fruits and kitchen supplies may make them hungry! On the other hand, when standing at the correct viewing angle, the smaller objects coalesce into an organized image, where object-level perception is ignored in favor of the broader information being presented: the image. The project demonstrates the power of perceptual organization in making a whole greater than the sum of its parts. 

When viewed from the correct angle, the player can see an obvious (albeit blurry/grainy) image of Lovett Hall on Rice campus.
